Gender parity is not a hero image with two hair textures. It is whether a barber opening the app for the first time sees workflows that assume clipper guards, not an afterthought tab labeled "men's."

We audit for balance before we ship.

## Language choices

Copy defaults to inclusive job titles — stylist, barber, artist, trainer — not assumptions about who sits in the chair. Pronouns follow member settings. Marketing lines are tested for whether they silently center one gender.

When we miss, we fix in place. No waiting for a "diversity release."

## Profile themes and visuals

Profile themes include palettes and typography requested by barbers, braiders, estheticians, and models — not one salon-pink template resized. Thumbnails, icons, and empty states are checked so no single gender presentation dominates the grid.

## Content equity

Education libraries are tagged by skill, not by gendered aisle. A skin chapter belongs to estheticians and barbers who do beard facials. Fitness modules do not treat strength as male and flexibility as female.

<PullQuote author="Idun Bergström">
  I have never seen barber education sit at the same depth as blowout education in one product. Here it does.
</PullQuote>

## Barbershops are not bolted on

Shop types — salon, barbershop, studio, gym, agency — change defaults: service menus, tax lines, chair language. A barbershop is a first-class tenant, not a skin.

## How we measure

Before major releases, we run a gender balance checklist: screens, examples, Advocate roster, and stock scenarios. We count who is centered, not only who appears.

Parity is maintenance. We intend to keep the receipts public in this journal when we fall short and correct course.
